Kim Louise Lemoine

Counterfeit government checks / identity theft

Source grade ACounty case file
At a glance · allegation, outcome, and later actions

What happened in this case?

Kim Louise Lemoine pleaded guilty to identity theft, forgery and grand theft. The 2009 court minutes record a five-year prison term with execution suspended and rehabilitation-center commitment; a 2015 expungement motion was denied.

What was alleged
State prosecutors described identity theft, forged checks, and grand theft in a counterfeit government-check scheme.
Plea or verdict
California DOJ reported guilty pleas; the supplied court docket lists the plea convictions and sentencing.
Sentence or restitution
July 2009 minutes record a five-year aggregate state-prison term with execution suspended and commitment to a rehabilitation center for up to five years. Restitution figures of $302,361 (2009) and $301,528.18 (2012) appear in different orders.
Later developments
The court denied an expungement motion in April 2015. The source does not explain the restitution difference or establish later relief.

Case background

The state Attorney General's sentencing announcement and a filed Sonoma court complaint provide government documentation. The complaint itself describes initial charges; the press release supplies the later conviction and sentence.

2009 sentencing detail

Source page 28 of the original Sonoma Superior Court portal export records a five-year aggregate state-prison term with execution of that sentence suspended, and a commitment to the California Rehabilitation Center for up to five years. This qualifies the shorthand description of a “five-year prison sentence” in the state press announcement: the portal entries do not establish that five years in state prison were actually served. The entry refers to other personal treatment-related matters that are intentionally not reproduced here.

Later court proceedings verified in the supplied docket

The Sonoma Superior Court case-detail export records a July 8, 2009 sentencing-minute restitution figure of $302,361, jointly and severally, to Bank of America (source page 28). Later courtroom minutes record a May 8, 2012 restitution determination of $301,528.18, jointly and severally with co-defendants (source pages 33–34). The source does not, by itself, explain why the two amounts differ or establish a current balance. This is a court-recorded restitution figure, not proof of an amount paid or currently outstanding. The April 30, 2015 courtroom minutes report that an expungement motion was denied and refer to outstanding restitution (source pages 34–35). This does not establish whether any subsequent relief occurred. Read the review log and its publication limits.
