People v. Constantine William Petros

The documented final disposition of Sonoma County Superior Court case SCR-715528-1.

Final recorded status: dismissedPenal Code § 1203.4September 17, 2020
At a glance · allegation, conviction, and later dismissal

What happened in this case?

Four insurance-related charges were dismissed, while one count produced a conviction that later received post-conviction dismissal.

What was alleged
The five filed counts alleged conspiracy, insurance-claim misconduct, and grand theft. Filing charges was not a finding that each allegation was true.
Plea or verdict
Four counts were dismissed in March 2017. A no-contest plea on the remaining insurance-claim count led to a conviction recorded in July 2017.
Sentence or restitution
Formal probation was imposed in 2017. The court record does not establish a jail or prison term for the dismissed counts.
Later developments
On September 17, 2020, the remaining count was reduced to a misdemeanor and dismissed under Penal Code §1203.4; probation was terminated. This was not an acquittal or a finding of factual innocence.

What the court record establishes

The 11-page public docket lists five counts. It records four dismissals in March 2017 and a no-contest plea on count 004. The docket records a conviction on that count in July 2017. On September 17, 2020, the court recorded a reduction to a misdemeanor and a dismissal under Penal Code § 1203.4, as well as termination of probation.

Important distinction: Penal Code § 1203.4 dismissal is a form of post-conviction relief. It is not an acquittal or a judicial finding of factual innocence, and it does not erase the historical fact that a conviction occurred. The accurate description is that the case ultimately received the dismissals recorded above.

  4. September 17, 2020

What this record does—and does not—prove

  • Proved by the displayed docket: Four counts were dismissed in 2017, and the remaining count was later dismissed in 2020 under § 1203.4.
  • Also shown: The remaining count previously resulted in a conviction following a no-contest plea. A later dismissal does not make that historical event disappear.
  • Not established by this docket: Whether a reported custodial sentence was actually served, or whether the court made any finding that the original allegations were false.
